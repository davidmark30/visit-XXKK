Spot Trading

Spot trading means buying or selling the actual cryptocurrency at the market price or at a price you specify.

For example, a BTC/USDT spot trade lets you exchange USDT for Bitcoin. If you buy 0.01 BTC, the Bitcoin is credited to your exchange balance after the order is filled.

Spot trading is generally easier to understand than leveraged derivatives because you are not opening a leveraged perpetual position. XXKK's own beginner material describes spot trading as direct buying and selling of assets such as BTC and ETH.

Futures and Perpetual Contracts

Futures or perpetual contracts work differently. Instead of simply buying the underlying coin, you open a derivatives position based on its price.

XXKK provides perpetual contract markets with real-time information including prices, 24-hour changes, highs, lows, and trading volumes.

Warning: Leverage can increase the size of a position relative to the capital used. It also increases the potential loss, so futures should not be treated as simply a faster version of spot trading.

What Is the Difference Between Maker and Taker Fees?

A maker order adds liquidity to the order book, while a taker order removes available liquidity by executing against existing orders.

On XXKK, the published standard spot schedule lists both maker and taker fees at 0.10%.

That does not make order type irrelevant.

A market order normally executes immediately against available liquidity. A limit order can give you more control over the price, although it may not fill.

For example, suppose BTC is trading around 60,000 USDT. You could:

  • Place a market order and accept the available prices.
  • Place a limit order and specify the maximum price you are willing to pay.
  • Use a limit order that rests on the order book and potentially behaves as a maker order.

The important distinction is that the trading fee and execution price are separate issues.

Futures also have another cost that spot traders do not normally deal with: funding.

Funding payments are associated with perpetual contracts and can move between traders according to the contract's funding mechanism. They should be treated separately from the maker or taker trading commission.

Example of Futures Fee Calculation

Suppose you open a 10,000 USDT futures position and your applicable taker rate is 0.06%.

The basic commission calculation is:

Calculation: 10,000 × 0.0006 = 6 USDT
That is the trading fee for the stated assumption.

It does not include potential funding, spread, slippage, or liquidation-related costs.

The distinction matters because a position can appear cheap based on the headline commission while becoming more expensive when held for a longer period.

What Is the Real Cost of a Crypto Trade on XXKK?

The real cost is usually a combination of several components:

Cost Formula:
Total cost ≈ trading fee + spread + slippage + applicable funding + withdrawal/network cost

Not every transaction has every component.

  • For a simple spot purchase that stays on the exchange, you may mainly deal with the trading fee and execution spread.
  • For a futures position, you may also have funding.
  • For a withdrawal, you have the applicable blockchain withdrawal charge.

Trading Fee

This is the explicit commission charged by the exchange when an order executes.

Spread

The spread is the difference between the best available buying and selling prices.

A tight order book can reduce this cost. A thin market can have a wider spread.

Slippage

Slippage occurs when your actual execution price differs from the price you expected.

It can become more noticeable when:

  • The market moves quickly
  • Your order is large
  • The trading pair has limited liquidity
  • The order book is thin

A market order does not guarantee a single execution price for a large order.

Funding

Funding applies to applicable perpetual futures positions. It is separate from the normal maker or taker commission.

Withdrawal Fee

A blockchain withdrawal can involve a network-related charge. XXKK's published withdrawal page warns that these fees can change without prior notice.

How Can You Reduce Your XXKK Trading Costs?

Reducing trading costs is not simply about finding the smallest percentage on a fee table. Execution quality matters too.

Compare the Complete Cost Before Ordering

Before pressing Buy or Sell, check the amount you will receive and the quoted execution price.

  • For fiat purchases, compare the quoted crypto amount with the amount you expected based on the current market price.
  • For spot trading, compare the best bid and ask and consider whether a limit order is more appropriate.

Avoid Unnecessary Trading

A 0.10% fee may look small on one transaction. Repeating trades many times can make the total much larger.

For example, 100 separate trades with 1,000 USDT of executed volume each represent 100,000 USDT of trading volume. At a hypothetical 0.10% fee, that is 100 USDT in commissions before considering spread and slippage.

The lesson is simple: turnover matters.

Use Limit Orders When Price Control Matters

A limit order lets you specify your acceptable price.

That can help when the market is volatile or when the spread is wider than usual. However, a limit order can remain unfilled, so speed and certainty may sometimes matter more than price control.

Watch Futures Funding

If you trade perpetual contracts, do not look only at the opening fee.

A position held over multiple funding periods can accumulate funding costs or receive funding depending on the applicable rate and position direction.

Check the Current Withdrawal Charge

If you frequently move funds to a personal wallet, compare the withdrawal cost before choosing a network.

For example, XXKK's published table shows different USDT withdrawal charges for TRC20 and ERC20.

Always confirm the current amount on the withdrawal screen because blockchain conditions and exchange policies can change.
